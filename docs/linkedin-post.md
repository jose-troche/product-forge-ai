# LinkedIn Companion Post

I didn’t build Product Forge AI just to generate product plans.

I built it to answer a harder question:

**What changes when AI stops acting like one assistant and starts working like a coordinated team?**

Give Product Forge AI an idea. It fans the work out to specialists across market research, product, UX, architecture, data, APIs, security, roadmap, KPIs, and risk. Their validated outputs come back together as one coherent, production-oriented proposal.

The interesting part is not the number of agents. It is the orchestration:

- Parallel execution with explicit dependencies
- Typed contracts between specialists
- Validation, retries, and graceful fallbacks
- Visible execution and targeted recovery
- Model choice per agent to optimize expertise, latency, or cost
- Synthesis that reconciles the whole system

That pattern extends far beyond product planning—to incident response, software delivery, research, compliance, customer operations, and strategic analysis.

Product Forge AI is both a working application and an experiment in how dependable multi-agent systems should be designed.
